# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Power-user gamers and enthusiasts who maintain large personal game libraries. They use Savepoint in focused sessions — cataloging purchases, updating play status, logging sessions, and mining filters and stats — often with dozens or hundreds of titles. Context: desktop or tablet at home, low-light environment, multitasking with Steam/GOG or a console nearby. Success means finding any game instantly, trusting the data (platform, year, playtime, completion), and feeling the app keeps pace with a serious collection without becoming busywork.

## Product Purpose

Savepoint (formerly GameVault) is a personal videogame library manager: add and edit titles, search and filter by platform/year/genre/developer, track completion and playtime, maintain a wishlist, and review play history. It exists to replace scattered spreadsheets and mental notes with one authoritative, fast collection tool. Success looks like daily or weekly use during backlog grooming, frictionless add/edit flows, and stats that reflect real play habits.

## Brand Personality

**The Save Point** — deep navy with one confident Savepoint blue, executed with tool-grade restraint. Blue is earned (primary action, selection, focus); cyan / amber / green / pink / slate are status tones only. A save-point crystal is the mark; Chakra Petch appears only on the wordmark and a few brand moments. Voice is direct and tool-like (Steam/GOG density + Linear keyboard fluency) with a light, game-literate wink in the moments that deserve it — empty states ("No save data found"), completions ("GG!"), saves ("progress saved") — never in labels or data. Emotional goal: calm ownership — "my progress is safe here."

## Anti-references

- Over-neon / RGB gamer cliché — constant glow, gradient text on everything, pulsing borders as default decoration
- Mobile free-to-play UI — loot-box energy, cartoon chrome, loud gradient CTAs, fake urgency
- Spreadsheet ugly — raw tables with no hierarchy, no scan path, no delight in the collection itself
- Generic SaaS dashboard — cream cards, hero metrics, eyebrow labels on every section

## Design Principles

1. **Density earns trust** — Power users expect Steam/GOG-level information richness; filters, stats, and metadata stay visible and scannable.
2. **Atmosphere serves focus** — Navy depth and the blue accent frame the collection; data and actions remain the hero, not the chrome.
3. **One primary task per screen** — Library browse, add/edit, profile, play history: each screen has a clear job; decorative motion never competes with the task.
4. **Contrast is non-negotiable** — Text readability (WCAG AAA where practical) beats atmosphere; muted grays and glow must not wash out body copy.
5. **Earn the gamer aesthetic** — Glow, the crystal and the display face are deliberate accents on brand moments, not a uniform skin. Buttons say what they do next.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.1 AA minimum; prioritize AAA-level text contrast on body copy and labels even when it softens accents.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`: replace glow/pulse/slide reveals with instant or crossfade transitions.
- Keyboard navigable forms, filters, and library actions; visible focus states on dark backgrounds.
- Placeholder and secondary text must meet contrast requirements, not default muted-gray-on-tinted-dark.
