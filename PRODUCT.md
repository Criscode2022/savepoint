# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Power-user gamers and enthusiasts who maintain large personal game libraries. They use GameVault in focused sessions — cataloging purchases, updating play status, logging sessions, and mining filters and stats — often with dozens or hundreds of titles. Context: desktop or tablet at home, low-light environment, multitasking with Steam/GOG or a console nearby. Success means finding any game instantly, trusting the data (platform, year, playtime, completion), and feeling the app keeps pace with a serious collection without becoming busywork.

## Product Purpose

GameVault is a personal videogame library manager: add and edit titles, search and filter by platform/year/genre/developer, track completion and playtime, maintain a wishlist, and review play history. It exists to replace scattered spreadsheets and mental notes with one authoritative, fast collection tool. Success looks like daily or weekly use during backlog grooming, frictionless add/edit flows, and stats that reflect real play habits.

## Brand Personality

**Refined Vault** — dark cyberpunk DNA executed with Apple-level restraint. Purple accent is earned (selection, primary actions, focus); cyan/pink/green stay semantic. Orbitron only for brand. Voice is direct and tool-like (Steam/GOG density + Linear keyboard fluency), not playful or childish. Emotional goal: calm ownership — "this is my vault," not a light show.

## Anti-references

- Over-neon / RGB gamer cliché — constant glow, gradient text on everything, pulsing borders as default decoration
- Mobile free-to-play UI — loot-box energy, cartoon chrome, loud gradient CTAs, fake urgency
- Spreadsheet ugly — raw tables with no hierarchy, no scan path, no delight in the collection itself
- Generic SaaS dashboard — cream cards, hero metrics, eyebrow labels on every section

## Design Principles

1. **Density earns trust** — Power users expect Steam/GOG-level information richness; filters, stats, and metadata stay visible and scannable.
2. **Atmosphere serves focus** — Neon and cyberpunk framing accent the collection; data and actions remain the hero, not the chrome.
3. **One primary task per screen** — Library browse, add/edit, profile, play history: each screen has a clear job; decorative motion never competes with the task.
4. **Contrast is non-negotiable** — Text readability (WCAG AAA where practical) beats aesthetic neon; muted grays and glow must not wash out body copy.
5. **Earn the gamer aesthetic** — Glow, gradients, and Orbitron are deliberate accents on headings and key actions, not a uniform skin over every element.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.1 AA minimum; prioritize AAA-level text contrast on body copy and labels even when it softens neon accents.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`: replace glow/pulse/slide reveals with instant or crossfade transitions.
- Keyboard navigable forms, filters, and library actions; visible focus states on dark backgrounds.
- Placeholder and secondary text must meet contrast requirements, not default muted-gray-on-tinted-dark.
